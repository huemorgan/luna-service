# Copy Guidelines

> How every word on luna.com.ai gets written. Read before writing any headline, subtitle, or section copy.

---

## The One Rule

**Speak to the reader's wish. Don't describe the product.**

The reader didn't come to learn what Luna *is*. They came hoping you'll solve something they want. Lead with the want. The features prove you can deliver it.

Bad: "An open-source AI agent platform with persistent memory and approval gates."
Good: "Finally, an AI that's actually yours."

Bad: "Deploy AI agents for your clients with per-client isolation."
Good: "Turn AI projects into a business that scales."

Bad: "Persistent memory. Real approvals. MIT licensed."
Good: "Get your hours back."

---

## Know What They Actually Want

Before writing copy for any page, write the reader's wish in one sentence. Not their problem. Their wish.

- **Agent Enthusiast** wish: *"I want an AI that's truly mine — smart, capable, and not going away."*
- **Service Provider** wish: *"I want to turn the chaos of client AI work into a real business."*
- **Business Operator** wish: *"I want my time back without losing control of how it gets done."*

Cross-ICP wish: **leverage they control.** Help that's theirs.

Every headline answers their wish. Not in features — in the language of their life.

---

## Voice & Tone

- **Direct.** No "Empowering you to..." No "Revolutionizing the way..."
- **Confident, not boastful.** "Yours forever" not "The world's most powerful agent platform."
- **Plain words.** "Remembers" not "leverages persistent context." "Asks first" not "implements consent gating."
- **Calm.** No exclamation marks. No "Get started today!" Just "Start free."
- **Honest.** If something isn't shipped, label it. If it's not perfect, say so. The reader can tell.

Luna's voice: a sharp friend who already built what you wanted, doesn't oversell it, and lets you try.

---

## Sentence Rules

1. **Cut adjectives.** "Powerful," "seamless," "revolutionary," "cutting-edge," "next-generation" — delete all of them. Show, don't claim.

2. **Use "you" and "your."** Cut "we," "our," "the platform," "users."
   - Bad: "We built persistent memory so users can have continuity."
   - Good: "Your agent remembers you."

3. **Verbs over nouns.** Action language.
   - Bad: "Memory persistence across sessions."
   - Good: "Remembers what you told it last month."

4. **Specific over abstract.** Numbers, names, comparisons.
   - Bad: "Affordable pricing."
   - Good: "$29/month. All models included."

5. **Short headlines.** 6 words or fewer for hero titles. 10 max for section titles.

6. **One idea per sentence.** If you used "and" twice, split it.

7. **Read it out loud.** If you'd never say it in conversation, rewrite it.

## Headline Patterns That Work

**The wish-as-statement:**
- "Finally, an AI that's actually yours."
- "Get your hours back."
- "Turn AI projects into a business."

**The challenge to the status quo:**
- "Chat windows forget you. This one doesn't."
- "Stop renting your AI."

**The promise:**
- "Yours to keep. Forever."
- "Asks first. Always."

**The question that answers itself:**
- "What if AI actually worked for you?"
- "Why don't you own your agent?"

---

## Headline Patterns That Don't Work

- **The category description:** "AI Agent Platform" / "Open-Source LLM Framework"
- **The feature list:** "Memory + Approvals + Plugins"
- **The corporate puff:** "Empowering teams to..."
- **The mystery:** "Imagine if..." / "What if..." (only works when it lands instantly)
- **The marketing cliché:** "Reimagine," "Unleash," "Transform," "Supercharge"

---

## Section-Level Copy Discipline

Every section answers one of three questions:

1. **What do I get?** (outcome for the reader)
2. **Why should I believe you?** (proof — screenshot, number, comparison)
3. **What's it cost me?** (price, effort, trade-off)

If a section answers none of these, cut it.

## The Apple Test (For Copy)

Apple doesn't write "MacBook Pro features Retina display with 254 PPI resolution and ProMotion adaptive refresh up to 120Hz."

Apple writes "**Liquid Retina XDR. Mind-blowing in every dimension.**"

Notice:
- No technical specs in the headline (they come later, as proof)
- It speaks to your experience, not the spec sheet
- Confident, declarative, short
- The product is implied — the wish is centered

Do the same for Luna.

---

## Checklist Before Shipping Copy

- [ ] Does the headline speak to the reader's wish (not Luna's feature)?
- [ ] Did I cut every adjective I could?
- [ ] Is "you" or "your" in the first sentence?
- [ ] Are there any banished words still in it?
- [ ] Is the hero title 6 words or fewer?
- [ ] Read aloud — would I actually say this?
- [ ] If I removed Luna's name, does the headline still pull the reader in?
- [ ] Does each section answer "what do I get?" / "why believe?" / "what's it cost?"
